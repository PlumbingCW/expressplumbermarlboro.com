When a tank starts leaking from the body, repair is off the table and replacement is the only fix. Emergency water heater replacement means removing the failed tank and installing a new one the same day when we can, so your household is not left without hot water for long.

This page covers how we decide on a replacement, the choices you have, and what a Marlboro homeowner should ask before approving the job.

Emergency replacement: when a failed tank has to come out

Water from the tank itself

Leaks from the tank body or base mean the steel has corroded through.

Rusty hot water

Brown or rust colored hot water can point to a tank rusting from inside.

A heater past its expected life

If the label shows an older unit that now needs frequent repairs, replacement is often wiser.

Water on the floor after the valve is tightened

A drip you cannot stop at a fitting may still be coming from the tank.

Choosing the replacement

Most homeowners replace like with like, a gas tank for a gas tank or an electric tank for an electric tank, because the connections are already in place. That is usually the fastest route in an emergency.

Other choices exist. A larger tank can suit a growing household, and a tankless or hybrid unit can change running costs. Switching fuel or type often takes planning, so we may suggest a temporary swap now and an upgrade later. Our tankless page explains that route.

What the replacement involves

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    We close the water, isolate power or gas, and drain the old tank.

  2. 2

    Remove and prepare

    The old unit comes out and we check the venting, shutoff and drain pan area.

  3. 3

    Install the new unit

    We connect supply, relief valve and discharge piping, then power or gas.

  4. 4

    Test and explain

    We check temperature, pressure and leaks, then show you the shutoff points.

Whether a permit or inspection applies depends on the type of work, so ask the Marlboro Township Construction Division at (732) 536-0200, extension 1800, or ask us, and we will tell you what we have seen required.

Sizing for Marlboro homes

The right tank size depends on how many people live in the house and how many bathrooms run at once. Many Marlboro homes have four or five bedrooms, and a tank that was fine for a small family may struggle during a busy morning.

We ask how many people use hot water and when, then match a tank's recovery rate to that pattern rather than just matching the old size.
