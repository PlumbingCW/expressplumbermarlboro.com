Cold showers on a winter morning make a water heater problem feel urgent, and a leaking tank really is. Emergency water heater repair means diagnosing why a tank or burner has stopped producing hot water and fixing the failed part, and we answer calls for Marlboro homes at any hour.

If water is pooling around the tank, shut off the cold water supply valve above it and call us. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 before anything else.

No hot water or a leaking tank: what to do first

  • ✓Water on the floor: close the cold water valve on top of the heater and turn off its power at the breaker or its gas control.
  • ✓Gas smell: leave the building, then call the gas utility or 911.
  • ✓No hot water but no leak: check the breaker or pilot, then call us if it does not restore heat.
  • ✓Rumbling or popping from the tank: sediment is building up and a service visit is wise.
  • ✓Hot water that runs out fast: a failed element or dip tube is a common cause.

What we check on a repair call

A tank water heater has a handful of parts that fail, and each one gives its own clue. On electric units we test heating elements and thermostats with a meter. On gas units we look at the pilot or igniter, the thermocouple, the gas control valve and the venting.

We also inspect the temperature and pressure relief valve, the anode rod and the drain valve, because a repair that ignores a worn safety part just sets up the next failure.

Why Marlboro water heaters wear out

A large share of Marlboro houses were built in the 1980s and 1990s, and many have the second or third tank since then. Large homes often run one big heater for several bathrooms, which means more heating cycles and more sediment settling at the bottom.

Marlboro Township's water comes partly from its own wells and partly from Middlesex Water Company, according to the township's 2025 Water Quality Report. If you want to know how your supply treats a tank, ask your water utility for its annual report and test your own tap.
