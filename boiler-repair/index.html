A boiler that quits in January is an emergency because the house loses heat and pipes can freeze. Emergency boiler repair means diagnosing why a hot water or steam boiler has stopped, restoring heat safely, and protecting the plumbing while you wait.

We respond to Marlboro homes at any hour. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 before calling us.

Why a boiler is treated as an emergency

This page sits with our emergency services because losing heat in winter threatens the house, not just your comfort. Water in cold pipes can freeze and split them, which turns a heating problem into a burst pipe problem.

If the house is getting cold, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, keep a small flow in vulnerable lines and call us right away.

Common reasons a boiler stops

No heat, no noise

Check the power switch, thermostat and gas supply. A tripped switch or a thermostat set wrong is the quick fix.

Banging or kettling

Mineral or sediment buildup in the heat exchanger can make a boiler bang.

Low pressure

A leak in the system or a failed expansion tank can drop pressure.

Pilot or igniter won't light

A failed igniter, flame sensor or gas valve can keep the burner off.

What we check

We start with safety, including carbon monoxide risk and venting. Then we check the thermostat call for heat, the burner, the circulator pump, the pressure and the expansion tank, and the zone valves that send hot water to different parts of the house.

A good diagnosis finds the failed part, not just the symptom. We also look at how the boiler is vented and whether it has a carbon monoxide detector nearby, because a heating appliance and a working detector go together.

Boilers in Marlboro homes

Marlboro has homes from several decades, and some older houses use hot water baseboard or radiators fed by a boiler, while many newer ones use forced air. If you have radiators or baseboard heat, a boiler is behind them.

A boiler works hardest on the coldest days, so that is when a weak part shows itself. If yours is older, ask about a service visit before the cold sets in.

You get the price before the repair starts, and if the boiler is near the end of its life, we explain that honestly.

Service before winter

A boiler that gets a checkup before the cold arrives is less likely to fail in the middle of it. A service visit covers the burner, the controls, the circulator, the pressure and the venting, and it is a chance to ask about the age of the unit.

If your boiler is old, ask us what parts are still available and what a failure would cost. Knowing that in October is easier than learning it in January.
