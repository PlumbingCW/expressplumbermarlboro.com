Basement waterproofing is about controlling where water goes before it reaches your living space. In Marlboro, where many homes have finished basements, the goal is to keep groundwater and surface water out or direct it to a pump, and to fix the cause rather than just the damp wall.

This page explains what causes basement water, the main waterproofing methods, and how we decide which fits your house.

Where basement water comes from

Water through the wall

Cracks in block or poured walls let groundwater push in.

Water at the floor edge

Pressure under the slab pushes water up at the joint between floor and wall.

Poor drainage around the house

Gutters, downspouts and grading that send water toward the foundation add to the load.

A plumbing leak that looks like seepage

Always rule out a pipe leak first. See our leak detection page.

Methods and what they do

MethodWhat it doesBest for
Gutter and grading fixesMoves roof and surface water awayWater entering after rain with no wall cracks

Crack repairSeals active cracks in wallsIndividual cracks leaking
Exterior waterproofingTreats the wall from outsidePersistent water through the wall, with access to dig

There is no single best method. A house with a downspout dumping against the foundation needs a different answer than one with a high water table. We look at the whole picture before recommending anything.

How we assess a wet basement

We look for the stains, efflorescence and cracks that show where water enters, and we check gutters, downspouts and grading outside. We also check for plumbing sources and look at any existing sump pit.

From there we explain the likely cause and the options in order, starting with the least invasive fix that will actually work. Sometimes the answer is a drainage repair outside, not an expensive system inside.

Why Marlboro basements take on water

A lot of Marlboro housing was built in the 1980s and 1990s, and foundations from that era are now decades old with settled soil, aging drain tile and aging gutter systems. Heavy rain and storm systems test all of it at once.

Finished basements raise the stakes. Carpet, drywall and stored belongings suffer when water arrives, and a mold problem can follow. Tackling the water source early costs far less than repairing the finished space later.

Before we visit a wet basement

  • ✓Note when the water appears, such as after rain, snowmelt or only in certain seasons.
  • ✓Photograph the stains and wet spots and the outside of the house on that wall.
  • ✓Check that gutters are clear and downspouts discharge away from the foundation.
  • ✓Move stored items off the floor and away from the affected wall.

Those four observations often tell us more than an hour of guessing. A basement that only leaks after a long rain usually has a drainage cause, while one that stays damp all year may point to a plumbing source or a high water table.
