What happens when you call our emergency line

Local plumbing knowledge

Why Marlboro plumbing fails the way it does

Marlboro Township grew in waves. Early growth came in the 1950s and 60s, a suburban boom followed after the early 1970s, and the 1980s and early 1990s brought large developments of four and five bedroom homes before estate homes took over. That means a big share of local houses are now 30 to 50 years old, with original water heaters long past their prime, aging shutoff valves and plenty of bathrooms sharing one set of drains.

When a house has several bathrooms, a second floor laundry and a finished basement, a single failed supply line or clogged main can reach a lot of rooms fast. This is why we ask every emergency caller where the main water shutoff is before anything else.

Water supply matters too. According to the Marlboro Township Water Utility Division's 2025 Water Quality Report, the township draws from five wells in the Potomac-Raritan-Magothy Aquifer and buys about three quarters of its supply from Middlesex Water Company. The report also states there are no lead service lines in the township's territory. Homes in Morganville, Robertsville and Wickatunk may also be on different arrangements, so ask your water utility which applies to your street.

If the leak is on the street side of your meter, it is the utility's responsibility. For an off hours water emergency, the township lists Marlboro Township Police at (732) 536-0100. Everything after the meter, from the service line into the house to the last faucet, is where we work.

Why call a pro

Fixing it yourself versus calling a plumber

Plenty of small plumbing jobs are fair game for a patient homeowner, such as replacing a faucet aerator or plunging a toilet. The trouble starts when a quick fix hides a bigger fault.

Common DIY risks

  • A sealed leak that keeps seeping inside the wall
  • Wrong fittings that fail after a few weeks
  • A drain cable that scrapes a cracked pipe
  • Shutoff valves that break when turned after years

What a plumber adds

  • A camera or listening tool to find the real cause
  • Parts matched to your pipe material and pressure
  • A tested repair before the wall or floor is closed
  • A clear price before work starts
