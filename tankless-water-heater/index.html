Tankless Water Heater in Marlboro, NJ

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows through the unit, so there is no tank to run out. We install, descale and repair tankless units for Marlboro homes, and we are straightforward about when tankless is a good fit and when a traditional tank still makes sense.

If you are weighing a switch, this page covers the supply requirements, the maintenance, and the common reasons a tankless unit stops delivering hot water.

Is tankless right for your house?

A big household

Several bathrooms and back to back showers are where continuous hot water pays off.

Limited space

A wall mounted unit frees up the floor area a tank occupied.

Supply limits

Tankless needs a larger gas line or substantial electrical capacity, so check before you commit.

Replacing an old tank

The swap is a natural time to upgrade if the venting and supply can handle it.

What a tankless installation needs

The main differences from a tank are the supply and the venting. Gas units burn more fuel at once, so the gas line must be large enough. Electric units draw heavy current, so the panel and wiring must be able to carry it.

Venting for gas tankless is specific to the model and must be installed as the manufacturer requires. We confirm all of this before quoting, because a unit that cannot be supplied properly will not perform.

Maintenance that keeps it running

Mineral scale builds up inside the heat exchanger and slows flow. Descaling clears it and restores performance, and many manufacturers recommend periodic flushing. How often depends on your water.

Marlboro Township's water comes from wells and from Middlesex Water Company, per the township's 2025 Water Quality Report. The report does not give a hardness figure we can quote, so test your tap or ask your water utility how hard your water is. Harder water means scale builds faster.

Common tankless problems

  • ✓Lukewarm water that never gets hot: scale or a failed sensor.
  • ✓Error codes on the display: look up the code, then call us for the fix.
  • ✓No ignition on gas units: gas supply, venting or igniter.
  • ✓Short bursts of hot then cold: flow rate too low for the unit to fire.

Sizing a tankless unit

Tankless units are rated by how much hot water they can deliver per minute at a given temperature rise. Winter groundwater is colder than summer, so a unit that handles a household in July may struggle in January.

We count the fixtures that run at once, such as a shower, a dishwasher and a washing machine, and size the unit to that load plus a margin. A unit that is too small will deliver lukewarm water at peak times.
