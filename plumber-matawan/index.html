Matawan is a compact borough with older homes close to the train station and two small lakes. A failed pipe or backed up drain here may sit in a house that is a century old. Calls to the emergency line are taken at any hour, and a licensed plumber leaves our Marlboro base for Matawan addresses.

Around the borough: Lake Lefferts, Lake Matawan and Main Street

Matawan became a borough in 1895, and the Aberdeen-Matawan train station dates to 1875. Homes near the station and the historic streets are among the oldest in our service area, with plumbing that has been patched and repiped over generations.

Lake Lefferts and Lake Matawan, created in the 1920s, sit near residential streets where basements can take on water after heavy rain. A failed sump pump or a blocked floor drain is a common emergency in lower lying parts of town.

Matawan water: wells in season, purchased water the rest of the year

The borough runs its own water system. The Middlesex Road Water Treatment Plant draws from two wells in the Old Bridge Sands Aquifer from April to mid October. From mid October through March, the borough buys water from New Jersey American Water, mostly from the Swimming River Reservoir.

For plumbing, a seasonal switch means water characteristics can change during the year. If you notice a change in taste, color or scale, ask the borough water department and check the latest annual report.
