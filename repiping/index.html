Repiping in Marlboro, NJ

When leaks keep coming back, new pipe ends the cycle. We repipe Marlboro homes in PEX or copper, whole house or in sections.

Repiping means replacing the old supply pipes in a house with new ones, either whole house or in sections. It is a bigger decision than a repair, and it makes sense when leaks keep returning, water looks discolored or pressure has faded because of corroded pipe.

This page explains the signs, the materials we use, what a repiping day looks like in an occupied home and how we keep the work as clean as possible.

Signs your house may need repiping

  • ✓Pinhole leaks that keep appearing in different spots.
  • ✓Rusty or brown water from several faucets.
  • ✓Low pressure throughout the house, not just at one fixture.
  • ✓Visible corrosion or crusty buildup on exposed pipe.
  • ✓Pipe material that is known to fail, such as old galvanized steel or polybutylene.

PEX or copper

MaterialStrengthsConsider
PEXFlexible, runs through walls with fewer joints, resists freeze splitting better than rigid pipeNot for outdoor exposure to sunlight, and must be installed to code
CopperLong track record and durableCosts more, and acidic water can wear it over time

Both are accepted materials. We explain which suits your house, your budget and the way your walls are built, and you choose.

Repipe all of it or only part

A partial repipe replaces the worst section, such as one bathroom group or the lines to a single wing. It suits a house where only part of the system has failed.

A whole house repipe replaces everything and avoids repeated repairs. If the pipes are the same age and material throughout, one pinhole leak is often followed by another, and a full repipe is the better value over time.

How the work goes

  1. 1

    Walk through and plan

    We look at every pipe we can reach and plan the route for new lines.

  2. 2

    Rough in the new system

    We run new lines, often through attics, basements and existing walls.

  3. 3

    Cut in and connect

    We swap the fixtures over and test the system under pressure.

  4. 4

    Patch and finish

    We repair access holes and clean up. Painting or finish work is not included unless we agree.

Repiping and Marlboro housing

Marlboro's suburban boom put up many homes in the 1970s through 1990s. Pipe materials from that period vary, and a house's original supply lines may now be several decades old.

Whether a repipe needs a permit and inspection depends on the work, so ask the Marlboro Township Construction Division at (732) 536-0200, extension 1800. We explain what we have seen required on similar jobs.

Living in the house during a repipe

Repiping takes planning but it does not mean leaving your house. We set a schedule for the days water is off, keep an area open for temporary use where possible and protect floors and furniture in the work areas.

Expect some noise and dust when we open walls or drill through framing. We contain it where we can and clean up at the end of each day. You see the schedule before work begins so you can plan around it.
