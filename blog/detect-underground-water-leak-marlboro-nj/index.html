Detecting an Underground Water Leak: Marlboro, NJ Guide

The warning signs, a simple meter test and how professionals pinpoint a buried leak.

By the Marlboro Express Plumbing team · Published November 10, 2026
24/7 emergency line
(732) 466-1098Call now

An underground water leak is a leak in the supply line between the street and your house, or in a pipe buried in the yard or under the slab. You cannot see it, but the signs show up in your water meter, your bill and the ground. If you suspect one, a meter test is the first and cheapest step.

This guide explains the warning signs, the meter test, how professionals pinpoint a hidden leak and what repair options exist.

Signs of a hidden leak

  • ✓A water bill that rises without any change in use.
  • ✓A meter that keeps moving when every fixture is off.
  • ✓A soggy patch, lush green strip or sinkhole in the yard.
  • ✓Reduced pressure at fixtures, especially after the main shutoff.
  • ✓The sound of running water when nothing is on.

The meter test

  1. 1

    Turn everything off

    Stop all water use inside, including ice makers and irrigation.

  2. 2

    Read the meter

    Note the dial and any small leak indicator, which is often a small triangle or wheel.

  3. 3

    Wait

    Leave the water off for an hour or two, or overnight.

  4. 4

    Read it again

    If the dial or indicator moved, water is going somewhere.

To tell whether the leak is inside or outside, close the main shutoff valve at the house and check the meter again. If it still moves, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and the house.

How a leak is located

MethodWhat it doesBest for
Acoustic listeningMicrophones pick up the sound of escaping waterPressurized lines
Pipe locatingTraces the route of buried pipePlanning the dig
Pressure testingIsolates sections to see where pressure dropsNarrowing the area
Thermal imagingShows temperature differences from waterSlab and wall leaks

Pinpointing matters because it means a small dig instead of a large one. Our leak detection page describes the process.

Repair options

Once the leak is found, repair depends on the pipe and its depth. A short section can be replaced after a small excavation. A long deteriorated line may be better replaced as a whole. Our water service line repair page explains how we approach each case.

Call 811 before any digging so that buried utilities are marked. Ask who owns which section of the line, because responsibility is often split between the utility and the homeowner.

What to do while you wait for a visit

If the leak seems large, close the main shutoff at the house and call us. For a small suspected leak, leave the water on, note the meter reading and avoid using water for a set period to confirm. Take photos of any wet ground, and write down the time you noticed it.

Keep children and pets away from soft or sinking ground. Do not dig near the line yourself, because buried gas, electric and communication lines may be close by.

Why a small leak is still worth fixing

  • ✓Constant leaks wash soil from under pipes and slabs.
  • ✓Water can enter a crawl space or basement and cause damp conditions.
  • ✓A leak wastes water and shows up on every bill.
  • ✓Pressure loss can affect appliances and fixtures.
  • ✓Waiting tends to turn a spot repair into a larger one.

A hidden leak does not fix itself, and the longer it runs the more soil it moves. Acting early usually keeps the repair small.

Reading your water meter

Most residential meters have a register that counts gallons or cubic feet and a small flow indicator that spins when any water moves. Find the meter in the basement, in a box near the street or in a utility closet. Clean the cover and look for the indicator.

If your utility reads the meter remotely, the display may show a flow flag as well. Ask the utility how to interpret it, because models differ.

What a leak survey records

A thorough survey ends with something you can keep. That includes the meter readings, the area where the leak was found, the pipe material and depth and photos where possible. This record helps with any repair quote and with conversations with the utility or your insurer.

Ask for it in writing. If a company gives only a verbal answer, you have little to compare against another bid.

Watch the bill

Compare your water bills month to month, adjusting for the season. A steady climb with no change in habits is one of the earliest signs of a hidden leak. Catching it early often keeps the repair small and the soil under your lawn intact.

Water supply facts for Marlboro homeowners

Marlboro Township's Water Utility Division serves much of the town from five wells in the Potomac-Raritan-Magothy aquifer, and about 75 percent of its supply is purchased from Middlesex Water, according to the township's 2025 Water Quality Report. The report also states there are no lead service lines in its territory.

If you see water coming up in the street or suspect the problem is on the utility side of the meter, call the Water Utility Division at (732) 536-0188. After hours, the township police can be reached at (732) 536-0100. For the private side, a professional leak survey is the next step.
