How to Install a New Garbage Disposal in Marlboro, NJ

The tools, the steps in order and the mistakes that cause leaks, plus when a plumber is the smarter choice.

By the Marlboro Express Plumbing team · Published November 10, 2026
24/7 emergency line
(732) 466-1098Call now

Installing a new garbage disposal is a job many homeowners can do in a couple of hours, and a few details make the difference between a unit that lasts and one that leaks in a month. This guide covers what you need, the steps in order and the points where a plumber is worth calling.

Whether you are replacing an old unit or adding one under a sink that never had it, start by turning off power at the breaker, not just the wall switch.

What you need before you start

  • ✓A disposal that fits your sink and a mounting kit that matches it.
  • ✓Plumber's putty or the gasket supplied for the flange.
  • ✓A power cord kit or access to a hardwired connection and an outlet switch.
  • ✓A bucket, rags, a screwdriver, a hex key and channel lock pliers.
  • ✓A flashlight and a way to keep the dishwasher drain hose out of the way.

Step by step

  1. 1

    Cut power and disconnect

    Switch off the breaker, confirm it is dead, then disconnect the old unit and the drain pipe.

  2. 2

    Remove the old mount

    Loosen the mounting ring, lift the unit away and remove the old sink flange.

  3. 3

    Seat the new flange

    Press putty under the new flange, set it in the drain opening and tighten the mount from below.

  4. 4

    Hang and wire the unit

    Lift the disposal onto the mount, connect the wiring and attach the drain outlet and trap.

Last, reconnect the dishwasher hose. On a new disposal the dishwasher inlet has a plastic knockout that must be removed with a hammer and screwdriver before the hose goes on. Skipping that step leaves the dishwasher unable to drain.

Run water, then test the unit for leaks at the flange, the drain joint and the dishwasher connection before you close the cabinet.

Avoiding the common mistakes

Forgetting the dishwasher knockout is the most frequent error. Overtightening the mounting ring and cracking the sink flange is the second. Using too much putty and squeezing it into the drain is a third, because it later breaks loose and clogs.

Choose a unit with enough power for your kitchen. A larger household that cooks daily will be better served by a stronger motor than a minimal one, and a cheap unit often jams more.

Choosing the right disposal

Disposals come in continuous feed and batch feed styles. Continuous feed runs while a switch is on and suits most kitchens. Batch feed only runs with the stopper in place, which some households prefer for safety around children.

Motor size matters more than most buyers expect. A small motor jams on fibrous scraps and struggles with a busy kitchen, while a larger one grinds more finely and runs quieter. Look at the noise insulation and the grind chamber material as well, since stainless steel parts resist corrosion better than coated ones.

Caring for the unit after installation

  • ✓Run a steady stream of cold water before, during and for about 15 seconds after grinding.
  • ✓Feed scraps in small amounts rather than one large load.
  • ✓Keep grease, coffee grounds in bulk, fibrous vegetables and bones out of the unit.
  • ✓Never put your hand inside, and use tongs or the hex key at the bottom to free a jam.
  • ✓Grind a few ice cubes now and then to knock loose residue from the chamber.

If the unit hums but does not spin, it is jammed, not dead. Turn off the power, work the hex key at the base back and forth and press the reset button on the underside. If it keeps tripping, call us before the motor burns out.

Local wiring and drain surprises

Older kitchens sometimes hide surprises under the sink. The drain may be a different size from the new disposal outlet, the trap may be too low for the new unit or the cabinet floor may have rot from slow leaks. Check these before you buy a unit, not after.

If the cabinet floor is soft, repair it first. A heavy disposal on a weak floor loosens joints over time, and the leak that follows is hard to trace to its cause.

Disposals in Marlboro kitchens

Marlboro kitchens are often large, with islands and second sinks, so the disposal under the main sink may be doing a lot of work. The township does not publish a rule we can quote on whether a disposal needs a permit, so permit rules vary.
