When Does a Water Heater Need Replacing? Marlboro, NJ Homes

The signs of a failing tank, how to find its age and how to choose between repair and replacement.

By the Marlboro Express Plumbing team · Published November 10, 2026
24/7 emergency line
(732) 466-1098Call now

A water heater usually needs replacing when it leaks from the tank, when repairs start piling up or when it no longer keeps up with your household. Age is a clue, not a verdict. Most tank heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, but water quality, maintenance and size all matter.

This guide lists the signs that point to replacement, how to read the age on your unit and how to decide between repair and a new heater.

Signs it is time to replace

  • ✓Water pooling at the base of the tank, which means the tank itself has failed.
  • ✓Rusty or discolored hot water, especially if it persists.
  • ✓Rumbling or popping sounds from sediment hardened at the bottom.
  • ✓Not enough hot water, or water that runs out quickly.
  • ✓Repeated repairs on the same unit.
  • ✓A unit older than the typical lifespan.

How to find the age of your water heater

The data plate on the side of the tank has a serial number. Many manufacturers encode the month and year in the first characters. A letter often stands for the month and the following digits for the year. Because formats differ, look up your brand's format or ask us to read it.

Take a photo of the plate. It also lists capacity, fuel type and the model you will need for parts or a replacement.

What to do if it is leaking right now

  1. 1

    Cut the power or gas

    Turn the breaker off or the gas control to off or pilot.

  2. 2

    Close the cold supply

    The shutoff valve above the tank stops more water from entering.

  3. 3

    Contain the water

    Mop up and move belongings away from the floor.

  4. 4

    Call for help

    Tell us the type of unit and where the water is coming from.

A leaking tank is an emergency because stored water can spill for hours and damage floors and walls below. Acting quickly limits the damage.

Safety checks that come with a new heater

A new water heater installation should include a temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, a shutoff on the cold supply and correct venting for gas units. Ask whether an expansion tank is needed on your system, and whether the old heater will be hauled away.

The installer should explain where the shutoffs are and how to set the temperature. A setting that is too high raises scald risk, and one that is too low can be a concern in a tank. Ask what setting they recommend for your household.

Planning ahead saves a cold shower

The best time to replace a water heater is before it fails. A planned replacement lets you choose the type, compare quotes and schedule the work. An emergency replacement happens on someone else's timetable and with fewer choices.

If your unit is old, ask for an assessment now. Knowing the condition helps you budget and avoids a rushed decision on a cold morning.

Keep the paperwork

Save the receipt, the model and serial number and the warranty terms for your new heater. Note the install date on the tank with a marker. When something fails years from now, this information tells you whether a warranty still applies and which parts you need.

Extending the life of a heater

Flush sediment from the tank once a year. Check the anode rod every few years, because it protects the tank from corrosion and wears out first. Keep the temperature at a safe setting, and make sure the relief valve works.

These steps cost little and often add years to a heater. If your water is hard, they matter even more.

Water heaters and water quality in Marlboro

Marlboro Township's water comes from township wells and from Middlesex Water Company, according to the 2025 Water Quality Report, and the report gives no single hardness figure we can quote for every address. Test your tap or ask your utility, because hard water leaves sediment in tank heaters and shortens their life.
